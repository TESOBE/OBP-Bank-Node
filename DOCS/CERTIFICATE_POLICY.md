# Certificate Policy

How participant certificates are issued, used, validated, revoked, and
rotated in an Open Corridor deployment. Companion to the register of
regulated entities; see also A1_A2.md (how certificates are presented on
API calls) and MESSAGE_SECURITY.md.

## 1. Principles

- **Authorization and certification are separate.** A bank participates
  because the regulator's register says so. A certificate only binds a key
  to a bank's identity; it never by itself grants access.
- **Keys never leave the bank.** Key pairs are generated by the bank (on
  the Bank Node host or an HSM). The CA receives a CSR, never a private
  key.
- **The register is the kill switch.** Removing a bank from the register
  excludes it at the next connection, independent of certificate lifetime.

## 2. Roles

| Role | Held by | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Registrar | Platform (pilot), admitting banks against the regulator's published list of licensed institutions; regulator directly (later), via OBP Regulated Entities endpoints operated for it | Authorizes banks onto the register; strikes them off |
| Issuing CA | Platform (pilot); regulator, national PKI, or commercial CA (later) | Verifies the requester is on the register, signs CSRs, publishes revocation status |
| Subscriber | Participating bank | Generates and protects keys, requests issuance/renewal, reports compromise |

## 3. Certificate profile

- X.509 v3, signed by the corridor issuing CA.
- Subject: `O` = legal name of the bank, `organizationIdentifier` =
  `bank_id` as it appears in the register (the join key between certificate
  and register).
- Key: EC P-256 or RSA 3072+.
- Extended key usage: `clientAuth`. One certificate per Bank Node instance.
- Validity: 12 months.
- A separate signing certificate (JWS message seals, QSEAL-analog) may be
  added later under the same policy; transport and signing keys are then
  distinct.

## 4. Issuance

1. The bank is entered on the register (prerequisite; no certificate is
   issued to an unregistered entity). During the pilot the platform makes
   the entry after verifying the bank against the regulator's published
   list of licensed institutions; later the regulator maintains the
   register directly.
2. The bank generates a key pair and CSR on its own infrastructure.
3. The CA checks the CSR's `bank_id` against the register and signs it.
   During the pilot this is part of the platform's onboarding flow.
4. The certificate and chain are returned to the bank; the certificate is
   pinned to the bank's API consumer at OBP-API.

## 5. Use and validation

- The Bank Node presents the certificate on every platform call: mutual TLS
  at the transport layer, or the `PSD2-CERT` header where TLS terminates
  earlier (A1_A2.md). Interface C (AMQP) runs over TLS with per-bank vhost
  credentials.
- The platform validates, in order: chain to the corridor root; match
  against the certificate pinned to the calling consumer (mismatch:
  `OBP-20210`); the bank's presence and status on the register.

## 6. Revocation and compromise

- Register strike-off is the primary control and takes effect on the next
  connection attempt.
- The CA additionally maintains certificate revocation status (CRL/OCSP)
  for key-compromise cases where the bank itself remains authorized.
- On suspected key compromise the bank requests revocation, generates a new
  key pair, and is reissued under §4. The old certificate's pin is replaced,
  which alone is sufficient to cut off the old key at the platform.

## 7. Renewal and rotation

- Renewal is self-service before expiry: new CSR, same `bank_id`, reissue
  under §4. Old and new certificates overlap for up to 30 days so nodes
  rotate without downtime.
- Rotation on demand follows the same path at any time.

## 8. Handover of the CA

The issuing CA can change without touching bank systems: the successor
(regulator, national PKI, or commercial CA) either takes custody of the
corridor root or cross-signs the platform's intermediate. Existing
certificates remain valid until natural expiry; new issuance moves to the
successor. The register, and therefore authorization, is unaffected.
